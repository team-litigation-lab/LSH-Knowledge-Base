## Opening a claim with the other driver's insurer

When you open a bodily injury claim with the third party's (3P) insurance company, or call to check whether a claim already exists, you are talking to the other side. The 3P carrier insures the person who caused the accident. Its adjuster's job is to investigate the claim and to keep what the company pays as low as the facts allow. Everything you say on that call can end up in the claim file.

So the rule is simple: **give the insurer what it needs to find the accident and the claim, and nothing about the client beyond that.**

This page covers what you may share, what you must never share, why, and how to handle the call when the adjuster asks for more.

## The rule at a glance

| | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ Share | The client's **first and last name** (no middle name) | It identifies the claimant, and it is on the police report. |
| ✅ Share | **Vehicle information**, such as the **license plate number** | It ties the client to the accident, and it is on the police report. |
| ✅ Share if they need it to find the claim | The client's **city and state** | Enough to tell two people with the same name apart. Never the street address. |
| ✅ Share if they need it to find the claim | A **phone number: the law firm's**, never the client's | Every call about the claim goes through the firm. |
| ❌ Never share | The client's **date of birth (DOB)** | It is a personal identifier, not a detail of the accident. |
| ❌ Never share | The client's **email address** | It opens a direct line to a represented client. |
| ❌ Never share | The client's **Social Security number (SSN)** | It is the most sensitive identifier there is, and it is never needed to open a claim. |
| ❌ Never share | The **vehicle identification number (VIN)** | Give the plate instead: it is enough to find the vehicle in the claim. |

**Why these and not others?** What you are allowed to share is the kind of information that is usually already on the police report, so the adjuster most likely has it anyway. You are confirming which accident and which claimant you mean, not handing over anything new.

## Why it matters

- **The client is represented.** Once the firm sends its Letter of Representation (LOR), the insurer deals with the firm, not with the client. Giving out the client's email or phone number invites the adjuster to contact the client directly.
- **Identifiers reach further than the claim.** A date of birth, an SSN or a VIN lets an insurer look much further into a person or a vehicle than the claim requires. The less the other side has, the less it can use to question the claim.
- **It protects the client's identity.** These details are exactly what identity theft is built on. They should leave the firm only when there is a reason, and opening a 3P claim is not one.
- **It is the firm's information to release.** What the insurer receives about the client's injuries, treatment, medical records or bills goes out from the firm, at the time the attorney decides (for example with the demand), not over the phone on the first call.

## Before you call

Have these ready from the client's file and the intake documents:

1. The client's first and last name, as written on the police report.
2. The date of loss (DOL) and where the accident happened.
3. The license plate number of the client's vehicle.
4. The 3P's name and, if the police report or the exchange of information gave it, the 3P's insurance company and policy number.
5. The police report or accident report number, if there is one.
6. The firm's phone number, and the firm's fax number or email address for correspondence.
7. The case notes: whether a claim was already opened, and any claim number or adjuster on file.

## On the call

**Open the call like this:**

> "Hi, this is [your name] from [the firm], calling on behalf of our client, [first and last name]. Your insured, [3P's name], was involved in an accident with our client on [date of loss] in [city, state]. I'd like to open a bodily injury claim, or check whether one has already been opened."

**If the adjuster needs more to find the claim,** give the plate number, the client's city and state, the police report number, and the firm's phone number as the callback.

**If the adjuster asks for something on the "never share" list,** decline politely and offer what you can give:

> "I'm not able to provide that. I can give you our client's first and last name and the license plate number from the police report. Please direct all communication about this claim to our office at [the firm's number]."

Keep it short and polite. You don't need to explain or apologize: this is the firm's procedure.

**If the adjuster asks about the client's injuries, treatment, a recorded statement or anything about fault,** don't answer it yourself. Say that the attorney's office will follow up, and pass the request to the attorney or case manager on the file.

## After the call

Write it down in the case notes, the same day, following the firm's note format:

- the claim number (or that no claim exists yet and one was opened),
- the adjuster's name, phone number, email address and fax number,
- any coverage information you were given (policy limits, coverage types),
- the date and time of the call and who you spoke with,
- any request the adjuster made that you passed on.

Then send the LOR to the 3P carrier, following the firm's LOR procedure and naming convention.

## 1P is a different call

This list is for the **third party's** insurer. When you contact the client's **own** insurer (1P), the client is that company's insured, and the carrier may need policy details to find the policy. Follow the firm's 1P procedure and the case notes. Even then, share only what the carrier needs to find the policy and the claim.

## Quick reference

- **Share:** first and last name · license plate · city and state · the firm's phone number.
- **Never share:** date of birth · email address · Social Security number · VIN.
- **Not yours to answer:** injuries, treatment, medical records, recorded statements, fault. Pass these to the attorney.
- **Always:** note the claim number and the adjuster's details, then send the LOR.
