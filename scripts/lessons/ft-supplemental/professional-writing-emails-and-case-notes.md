## Your writing is the firm's record

In a law firm, almost everything you write lasts. An email to an adjuster becomes part of the claim file. A case note is what the next person reads before they call the client. A message to a provider may be shown to the provider's billing manager, and in some situations case notes and correspondence can be read by a judge, a mediator or the other side. When a client complains, the notes are how the firm proves what was done and when.

So professional writing in a law firm has two jobs: **to communicate clearly now**, and **to leave an accurate record for later**.

This article covers emails to the four groups you will write to most (clients, adjusters, providers and attorneys) and the case-note standard that runs through the whole Foundational Training, from Law Firm Communication (Days 1–2) to Reception (Days 3–5), Intake (Days 7–9), Insurance Communication (Days 10–12) and Provider Communication (Days 13–16).

## Five rules for everything you write

1. **Lead with the point.** Say what you need or what happened in the first two sentences.
2. **Identify the case every time.** Client name, date of loss (DOL) and, for insurers, the claim number. The reader may handle hundreds of files.
3. **Facts, not opinions.** Write what was said and done, not what you think about it.
4. **One topic per email.** Separate requests get separate emails, so each can be tracked and answered.
5. **Read it once more before you send.** Check the recipient, the client name, the attachments and the numbers.

## Subject lines

A good subject line lets anyone find the email months later. Use the pattern your firm sets; if there isn't one, this works:

**[Client name] | DOL [date] | [Claim no. if any] | [What it is]**

| Recipient | Example subject line |
|---|---|
| Adjuster | `Maria Lopez · DOL 03/14/2026 · Claim 0123456789 · Letter of Representation` |
| Provider | `Records and Bills Request · Patient: Maria Lopez · DOB on file · DOS 03/14/2026–present` |
| Client | `Your case update · Maria Lopez` |
| Attorney | `Lopez (DOL 03/14/2026): 3P offer received, $8,000, needs response by 11/07` |

Avoid empty subject lines and vague ones like "Question", "Follow up" or "Documents". For providers, follow the firm's procedure about whether to include the date of birth: many firms keep it out of the subject line and put it only in the request or the authorization.

## Tone

Professional, polite and neutral. Not stiff, not chatty.

- **Be courteous even when the other side isn't.** An email to an adjuster may be read aloud at mediation. Never sound angry, sarcastic or threatening.
- **Be warm with clients**, but clear. Many clients are in pain, stressed and unfamiliar with the process.
- **Avoid slang, emojis and text-speak** in work emails.
- **Avoid legal jargon with clients.** Write "the insurance company for the other driver" rather than "the 3P carrier", or explain the term the first time.
- **Use the person's name and title.** "Dear Ms. Johnson" or "Hi Ms. Johnson" for a first contact; follow their lead after that.

## What never goes in writing

- **Promises about results:** "You'll definitely get your bills paid", "This should settle for at least $50,000", "Your check will be here Friday." Only the attorney discusses value or outcomes, and dates depend on things outside the firm's control.
- **Legal advice** to anyone (see *Unauthorized Practice of Law*).
- **Opinions about the client, the case or the other side:** "The client seems to be exaggerating", "This adjuster is lazy", "Weak case."
- **Admissions of fault** about the accident, the client or the firm.
- **Information the recipient doesn't need.** A provider doesn't need the accident story; a 3P adjuster doesn't need the client's DOB (see *Opening a 3P claim*).
- **Anything you wouldn't want read out in court.**

## Emails by recipient

### To clients

Short, plain and kind. Tell them what is happening, what they need to do (if anything) and when they will hear from the firm next.

> **Subject:** Your case update · Maria Lopez
>
> Hi Ms. Lopez,
>
> I'm following up on our call today. We have sent the records request to Sunshine Orthopedics and will follow up with them next week.
>
> **What we need from you:** please send us a photo of the front and back of your health insurance card by Friday, November 7. You can reply to this email or text it to our office at [firm number].
>
> If you have any new appointments or receive any bills, please let us know.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name]
> [Title], [Firm name]
> [Firm phone]

### To adjusters

Formal, factual and specific. Include the claim number, attach what you mention and say exactly what you are asking for.

> **Subject:** Maria Lopez · DOL 03/14/2026 · Claim 0123456789 · Request for policy limits disclosure
>
> Dear Mr. Carter,
>
> Our firm represents Maria Lopez for injuries from the accident on March 14, 2026, involving your insured, James Reed. Our Letter of Representation is attached.
>
> Please provide a disclosure of your insured's bodily injury policy limits and the declarations page.
>
> Please direct all communication about this claim to our office and do not contact our client directly.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name], [Title], on behalf of [Attorney name], Esq.
> [Firm name] · [Firm phone] · [Firm email]

Never discuss injuries, treatment or value in an email to an adjuster unless the attorney has told you exactly what to send.

### To medical providers

Clear about the patient, the dates of service and exactly what you need. Attach the signed authorization.

> **Subject:** Records and Bills Request · Patient: Maria Lopez · DOS 03/14/2026–present
>
> Dear Records Department,
>
> Our firm represents your patient, Maria Lopez, in a personal injury matter. Please provide the following for dates of service **03/14/2026 to present**:
>
> - Complete medical records
> - Itemized billing statement (with CPT codes)
>
> The patient's signed HIPAA-compliant authorization is attached. Please send the records to [firm fax / email / portal] and let us know if there is a fee.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name], [Title], [Firm name], [Firm phone]

### To attorneys and supervisors

Busy attorneys read the first line and decide whether to read the rest. Put the case, the issue and what you need from them up front.

> **Subject:** Lopez (DOL 03/14/2026): 3P offer received, $8,000, needs response by 11/07
>
> Attorney Smith,
>
> **Need:** your instructions on the 3P offer.
>
> - Adjuster: Tom Carter, ABC Insurance, (555) 555‑0100
> - Offer: $8,000, full and final, made by phone today at 2:15 pm
> - Adjuster asked for an answer by 11/07
> - Medical specials so far: $14,250 (from the meds tab, not yet complete)
>
> I told the adjuster I would pass the offer to you and made no comment on it. Noted in the file.
>
> [Your name]

## The case note standard

Every call, email, text, voicemail and task gets a note in the case management system, the same day, ideally right after it happens. A good note answers six questions:

| Element | What to write | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Date and time** | When it happened (if the system doesn't stamp it) | 11/01/2026, 10:40 am |
| **Who** | Name, role and organization, and how you reached them | Called Tom Carter, adjuster, ABC Insurance, (555) 555‑0100 |
| **Why** | The purpose of the contact | To confirm the 3P claim and send the LOR |
| **What was said** | The facts, briefly; exact figures and dates | Claim open under 0123456789. Liability still under review. Adjuster requested medical records; told him the attorney will send them when ready |
| **Outcome** | What was done or agreed | LOR sent by email 10:55 am |
| **Next step and due date** | What happens next, who does it, by when | Follow up on liability decision 11/15/2026 (task created) |

**A good note:**

> 11/01/2026 10:40 am. Outbound call to Tom Carter, adjuster, ABC Insurance, (555) 555‑0100, re: 3P BI claim. Confirmed claim 0123456789 open; liability under investigation. Adjuster asked for med records; told him the attorney will provide records when ready, gave no details on treatment. Sent LOR by email at 10:55 am (saved to Correspondence). Next: follow up on liability decision 11/15/2026; task assigned to me.

**A weak note:**

> Called adjuster, he was rude. Will call back.

The weak note doesn't say who, which claim, what was learned or when the follow-up is due, and it includes an opinion.

### Facts versus opinions

- Write **"Client said she missed three appointments because she had no ride"**, not "Client is non-compliant."
- Write **"Adjuster said the claim is under review and declined to give a date"**, not "Adjuster is stalling."
- Write **"Client was crying and said the pain is worse"**, not "Client is dramatic."

Use quotation marks when the exact words matter, such as an offer, a denial or a client saying they want to drop the case.

### Other note habits

- **Note voicemails and attempts too:** "Called client at (555) 555‑0123, no answer, left voicemail asking for a call back." Attempts show the firm kept trying (and support Rule 1.4 communication).
- **Note where documents were saved** using the firm's naming convention (see *Naming Documents and Case Folders*).
- **Always create the follow-up task** in the system, not only in your head or in the note.
- **Correct, don't delete.** If a note is wrong, add a new note correcting it, unless the firm's procedure says otherwise.
- **Use standard abbreviations only** (DOL, DOS, LOR, BI, PIP, UM). Avoid private shorthand others won't understand.

## Common mistakes

- **Wrong recipient or wrong client name.** The most damaging and most common error. Check before sending.
- **Missing claim number or DOL** in emails to adjusters and providers.
- **Long emails with several requests**, where one gets missed.
- **Promising dates or results** to clients ("Your check should come next week").
- **Opinions and emotions** in notes or emails.
- **Notes written days later** from memory, or not at all.
- **No next step or due date** in the note, so nobody follows up.
- **Replying-all** to a thread that includes people who shouldn't see the reply.
- **Forgetting the attachment** you said was attached.

## Quick reference

| Task | Checklist |
|---|---|
| Any email | Right recipient · client name · DOL / claim no. · one topic · attachments · polite and neutral · signature with firm phone |
| Email to a client | Plain words · what's happening · what they need to do, by when · when they'll hear next · no promises |
| Email to an adjuster | Formal · claim no. · LOR attached or referenced · specific request · no injury details unless approved |
| Email to a provider | Patient · dates of service · exactly what you need · authorization attached · where to send |
| Email to an attorney | Case + issue + what you need in the subject and first line · facts in bullets · deadline |
| Case note | Date/time · who · why · what was said · outcome · next step + due date · facts only |
