## Why you need to know the carriers

On almost every intake call, the caller will name at least one insurance company: their own, the other driver's, or both. You need to know which is which, and to write each name down exactly.

- **1P or 3P?** The client's own insurer is the first party (1P). The at-fault driver's insurer is the third party (3P). The firm opens different claims with each, and a 3P call follows different rules (see *Opening a 3P claim* under See also). If you mix them up in the notes, the claims specialist calls the wrong company.
- **Spell it exactly.** "United Auto" is not "United Healthcare". "Liberty Mutual" is not "Liberty National". Write the carrier name exactly as it is printed on the card, letter or police report. If there is no document yet, write what the caller says, mark it "per caller, not verified", and ask for a photo of the card.
- **Parent companies and brands.** Large insurers sell through several companies and brands. A caller may say "Allstate" while the card says "National General", or say "GEICO" while the card says "GEICO Indemnity Company". Both are correct. Note the brand the caller gives *and* the company name printed on the card, because the printed name is the one on the policy.

## How to find the right carrier and claims contact

Callers often don't know their carrier's legal name, or the other driver's carrier at all. Ask for the documents, in this order:

1. **The insurance ID card.** It shows the company name, the policy number, the named insured, the policy dates and, often, a claims phone number. Ask the caller to read it out, or to send a photo of both sides.
2. **The declarations page (dec page).** The summary page of the policy. It names the exact underwriting company, the vehicles, the coverage types and the limits. This is the best source for 1P coverage.
3. **The police report.** Its insurance section usually lists each driver's carrier and policy number. This is often the only way to identify the 3P carrier at intake.
4. **The adjuster's letter or email.** If a claim is already open, the letter gives the claim number, the adjuster's name and direct contact details, and sometimes a separate mailing address for documents.

Two things to watch for:

- **Regional offices.** Large carriers handle claims from regional or state offices. The letter may come from a different city or company name than the card. That is normal; copy the details from the letter.
- **Third-party administrators (TPAs).** Some policies, especially commercial, rental-car and self-insured fleets, have their claims handled by a separate company. If a letter comes from a company the caller has never heard of, it may be a TPA working for the insurer. Note both names.

## What to collect about insurance on an intake call

Ask for every item below. If the caller does not know, write **unknown**. Never guess a carrier, a policy number or a coverage type.

- **1P carrier:** the client's own insurer, as printed on the card.
- **3P carrier:** the at-fault driver's insurer, from the exchange of information or the police report.
- **Policy number** for each policy.
- **Named insured:** the person or business who bought the policy (it may not be the driver).
- **Claim number**, if a claim is already open with either carrier.
- **Adjuster:** name, phone, email, for each open claim.
- **Coverage types**, if known: bodily injury (BI), property damage (PD), personal injury protection (PIP), medical payments (MedPay), uninsured motorist (UM), underinsured motorist (UIM). Write unknown for each one the caller cannot confirm.
- **Recorded statement:** has the client already given one to any insurer, and to which one? Note yes, no or unknown.
- **Health insurance:** the health plan, and whether the client has **Medicare** or **Medicaid**. These can have repayment rights out of the settlement, so the firm needs to know at the start.

## Non-standard, rideshare and commercial policies

- **Non-standard carriers** (UAIG is one) insure higher-risk drivers, often at state-minimum limits. Expect low BI limits, and flag the case for a UM/UIM check on the client's own policy.
- **Rideshare** (Uber, Lyft and delivery apps): which policy applies depends on the driver's app status at the moment of the crash: app off (the driver's personal policy), app on and waiting for a ride (a lower company policy), or a ride accepted or passenger on board (much higher company coverage). Note the app, the driver's status if known, and every carrier named.
- **Commercial vehicles** (work trucks, company cars, buses): the employer's commercial policy may apply if the driver was working. Note the business name on the vehicle, whether the driver appeared to be working, and any carrier or TPA named on the police report. If the vehicle belongs to a city, county, state, school district or transit agency, flag it to the case manager/attorney the same day: government claims have short notice deadlines.

In all three, collect what you can and pass it to the case manager. You don't decide which policy pays.
