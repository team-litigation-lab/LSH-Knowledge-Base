*A simple guide to the property damage portion of a car accident claim, and how the information you collect is used at each stage. It is written for Florida; the same steps apply in most states, but the rules and forms differ, so confirm anything unclear with the handling attorney.*

When a car accident happens, several key steps are taken to evaluate, document and resolve the property damage portion of the claim. This page walks through those steps in order.

## The six steps at a glance

| Step | The question it answers | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Intake** | What happened? | Collect the essential details and open the property damage claim. |
| **2. Liability** | Who may be responsible? | The adjuster reviews the evidence; you keep the facts organized and the claim coordinated. |
| **3. Insurance and coverage** | Which insurance applies? | Verify the policies and what they cover. |
| **4. Vehicle damage** | How much will it cost to repair? | Document the damage and get a repair estimate. |
| **5. Repair or total loss** | Can the vehicle be repaired? | Follow the repair process or the total loss process. |
| **6. Documentation and follow-up** | What is needed to finalize the claim? | Collect the final documents and keep the file moving. |

## 1. Intake: what happened?

We begin by collecting the essential details about the accident to open the property damage claim.

**Key information gathered:**

- Your vehicle information
- Parties involved
- Details of how the accident happened
- Police or accident report
- Insurance information (your policy and the other party's)
- Photos and any available documentation

**Skill highlight:** information gathering, organization and claim setup.

## 2. Liability: who may be responsible?

After the claim is submitted, the insurance company assigns an adjuster to investigate and determine liability based on the available evidence.

**Factors reviewed:**

- Your version of the accident
- The other driver's version
- The police report narrative
- Photos of the vehicles and damages
- Damage locations and patterns
- Other supporting documentation

**Skill highlight:** fact organization and claim coordination.

## 3. Insurance and coverage: which insurance applies?

The claim involves reviewing the relevant insurance policies and coverage that may apply to the property damage.

**Coverage that may be involved:**

- Your policy (for example, collision coverage and deductible considerations)
- The other driver's property damage liability coverage
- Policy limits and coverage details
- Any additional applicable coverage

**Skill highlight:** insurance verification and coverage coordination.

## 4. Vehicle damage: how much will it cost to repair?

Once coverage and liability issues are addressed, the vehicle damage is documented and evaluated.

**Damage evaluation includes:**

- Vehicle photographs
- A virtual estimate or an in-person inspection
- A repair estimate prepared by an appraiser or authorized source
- Review of visible and related damages

**Skill highlight:** damage documentation and repair coordination.

## 5. Repair or total loss: can the vehicle be repaired?

Based on the estimate and the extent of the damages, the claim will follow either a repair process or a total loss process.

| | **Repair process** | **Total loss process** |
|---|---|---|
| **When** | If repairable | If considered a total loss |
| **What happens** | Schedule repairs at a repair facility. Rental vehicle may be provided (if applicable). Repairs are completed and the vehicle is returned to you. | The vehicle's value is evaluated. The total loss process is initiated. Settlement and next steps depend on the policy, the damages and other circumstances. |

**Skill highlight:** case analysis and repair or total loss coordination.

## 6. Documentation and follow-up: what is needed to finalize the claim?

After repairs are completed (or the total loss process is resolved), final documents are collected and the claim is updated accordingly.

**Final documentation may include:**

- Property damage estimate
- Photos of the vehicle
- Repair invoices
- Proof of payment
- Rental information (if applicable)
- Any additional claim-related documentation

**Skill highlight:** case management and follow-through to keep the file moving.

## The takeaway

Behind every property damage claim is a process of gathering information, verifying coverage, coordinating with insurers, documenting damage and keeping the file moving.
