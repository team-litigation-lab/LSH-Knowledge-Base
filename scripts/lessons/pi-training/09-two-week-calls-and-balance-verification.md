## 2-week calls

Every two weeks during treatment, contact the client (see the *Case Phases Procedure*). If the client says they are already treating, ask how their treatment is going, and when their next appointment is. This also helps confirm they are treating consistently.

Make sure **all** providers are included and documented in the Medical section of the case system. A provider needs to be included even if the client treated at a specific location only once. We need all the **itemized** bills and medical records from each provider the client treated with. If we do not know the provider's name, enter it as "Unknown - Chiropractor" or "ER Physician - Unknown". **Every provider needs to be in the case system.**

If the client went to the ER, ask how they got there: by EMS, or did someone take them? If by EMS, request the **EMS bill**.

### When you speak with clients about treatment, ask

1. How is treatment going? Have you made every visit so far?
2. When was your last visit?
3. When is your next visit?
4. Have you had any diagnostic testing, such as MRIs, CTs or X-rays?
   - If so, where? If the client has not had an MRI, tell them it is very likely they will be referred out for one within the first 2 to 3 weeks. **Every case should have an MRI of the body parts the client complains about.** The MRIs show potential internal injuries the client might not be aware of, and they also increase the value of the case. Personal injury chiropractors know this and usually refer clients out quickly.
   - We also need to know exactly where the client had the MRI so we can request the records and bills and check whether it was a **positive MRI finding**. Positive findings help increase the value of the case. If the client has a positive finding, they are usually referred out to an orthopedic or neurology specialist, depending on the body part injured.
5. Has the doctor sent you to any other providers? (Think of situations where one clinic refers clients to see an MD at another.)
6. Send us any bills you get in the mail. Remind clients that if they have health insurance, they can and should give it to the doctors, especially if they went to the ER. (Health insurance may or may not need to be paid back, depending on whether it is an ERISA qualified plan. Health insurance companies are able to get large reductions. At the end, clients benefit financially if health insurance pays.)
7. Make sure the client knows to tell us **any time** they are sent to a different facility by doctors. Getting the health insurance EOB also helps us find out exactly where and with whom the client treated. If there is no health insurance, we need to review the medical records thoroughly, and we may also need to call the providers to check whether they have different billing.

**Remember to have the clients verify the providers.** It is VERY important that we have all providers and all itemized bills and medical records before moving the case to ready for demand. We do not want to send the demand out if we do not have all of them (unless there is an exception, such as needing to supplement, medical bills higher than the limits, or a limits issue, case by case).

## Balance verifications

Balance verifications help make sure we do not miss a provider, and let us know whether there is health insurance, possible liens, or bills sent to collections.

When verifying balances, confirm **verbally or by email**:
- Who called whom, who we spoke with, and the phone number we called.
- The **original balance** (this confirms the bill we have is the updated and accurate one).
- The **payments made, and by whom**. For example: "Health Insurance - Cigna paid $110." This also helps confirm whether the client has used health insurance. Ask for the name of the specific insurance company that paid.
- The **adjustments made**.
- The **current balance due**.

**If the account has been sent to collections or bought by a funding company**, we need the name of the new provider, the account number and the phone number. Contact the new company and verify the balance with them. This is very important so that once the case settles we send the payment to the correct provider. **The case should not move to the next phase until this is done.** If there is a special circumstance, such as needing to send the demand and supplement later, document it.

### Other things to confirm

- **Health insurance EOB.** Request it to confirm whether health insurance made payments. It also shows whether the client treated with an additional provider we may not know about. Confirm whether the plan is an **ERISA qualified plan**: the policy language tells you, or you can call the health insurance subrogation specialist. *Hint: Medicaid, Medicare, VA and railroad plans are ERISA plans and have to be paid back.*
- **Med pay EOB.** Request it to confirm that med pay has been exhausted. Confirm on all cases whether there is med pay. Some insurers show it on the dec page and others do not. If it is not on the dec page, call the UM adjuster and ask.
- **All providers.** As soon as we know the client has completed treatment, confirm all providers. You can email the client all the providers and dates of service before the case changes to Pre-Demand, to confirm we have not missed one. All providers must be on the demand so we do not settle cases for less than they are worth.
- **Call before the demand.** The attorney or case manager calls the client before sending the demand to verify information and give an update, so the client knows the process and has realistic expectations. The attorney or case manager also double-checks that the limits are correct before the demand goes out.
